**Abstract**::
> We’re buzzing, anxious and stressed because we’ve created a world our bodies can’t exist in. Nearly half of the US population reports their lives are affected by anxiety, stress, or burnout. Anxiety is everywhere. But here’s the thing: Anxiety isn’t the problem. The problem is that we are unsafe, disconnected, unhealthy and living like we have no say in what happens next. In the highly anticipated follow-up to Redefining Anxiety, Dr. John Delony walks you through the Six Daily Choices you need to make to build a non-anxious life. These include:
> - Choosing Reality
> - Choosing Connection
> - Choosing Freedom
> - Choosing Health and Healing
> - Choosing Mindfulness
> - Choosing Belief
>
> If you create a life of intentionally living out the six choices outlined in this book, you’ll be able to better respond to whatever life throws at you and build a more peaceful, joyful, non-anxious life.

**Thoughts**:: Overall, I enjoyed the book. I am definitely not the target audience, but there are many thoughts, ideas, and advice that are applicable to general living. Perhaps we can all take steps to "build a non-anxious life?"

The sections that stood out to me most were Choosing Freedom and Choosing Mindfulness. Freedom really resonated with me just in general, as freedom and independence are some of my core tenants. I felt the whole world could afford be more Mindful, including myself. I also enjoyed Choosing Belief, which is something I already try to incorporate into my life. Dr. Delony echoed many thoughts I have been having lately, especially about technology and health. I feel reading this book with my wife has helped us to establish more concrete goals and has helped us to self-identify where we could make improvements both for ourselves and for our family.

I do feel Dr. Delony was a bit repetitive in some places, in particular the introductory chapters leading up to the core "Choosing" chapters. This did not detract from the overall reading experience though. Towards the beginning, Dr. Delony makes a statement along the lines of, "Anxiety is not the problem. The way we're living is." I understand what he's attempting to get across here: the way we live is what causes anxiety to explode. I also understand some people need a kick in the pants to get going. However, I am concerned certain individuals in my life with genuine, diagnosed mental health disorders may take this statement too harshly and not reap the benefits of the rest of the book. There are still many treasure to be had, even if one has to adjust them for their own needs, and I'd hate to see someone turn away or harden their hearts due to a potentially overly-harsh or off-putting thesis statement that wasn't meant to attack individuals with imbalances. Nevertheless, the book is worth the read and perhaps reread to remind oneself of how they can improve.
