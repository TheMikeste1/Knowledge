> [!QUOTE] p. 61 Murder in Trafalgar Square
> Jacques shoots his uncle dead in Trafalgar Square and is apprehended on the spot by Sherlock; Tom reads about is in the *Guardian*, and Boris learns of it in *Pravda*. Now Jacques, Sherlock, Tom, and Boris have had remarkably different experiences-- to say nothing of their earlier biographies and future prospects-- but there is one thing they share: they all believe a Frenchman has committed murder in Trafalgar Square.
^Murder-in-Trafalgar-Square

## Thoughts
- The important part of [[#^Murder-in-Trafalgar-Square|Murder in Trafalgar Square]] is everyone believes the same thing
- We can learn the same information through different experiences
- This indicates our brains can extract the "aboutness," or meaning, behind things
- Our brains see meaning everywhere-- we're hardwired to look for it
- We don't necessarily "learn" or "come up with" meaning. We usually just "know" it.
    - E.g., "Chairs are larger than shoes" [p. 63]
        - We *just know* this is (generally) true
- [[#^hearing-aid-salmon]] Some things we already know/believe without learning them explicitly
    - We *intuitively* know these things
    - However, we still must be familiar with the ideas and vehicles (words, symbols, etc.) around the meaning
        - A dog might understand someone was killed, but have no idea what a Frenchman is
- It is difficult to engineer things to understand meaning
    - [[LLM]]s and [[Jev]] are probably the closest we have right now
