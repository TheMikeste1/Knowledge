> [!QUOTE] p. 73
> [Folk psychology is] the *talent* we all have for interpreting the people around us-- and the animals and the robots and event the lowly thermostats-- as *agents* with information about the world they act in (*beliefs*) and the goals (*desires*) they strive to achieve, choosing the most *reasonable* course of action, given their beliefs and desires.
^folk-psychology-definition

> [!NOTE]
> This is the modern definition. It's not the same as the older *Volkpsychologie*

## Thoughts

- [[#^folk-psychology-definition]] Specifically the natural, or *intuitive*, talent-- not something trained nor studied
    - "Folk physics" is the talent for knowing how general physics works
    - We just naturally know these things
        - "You must be thirsty!"
        - "Someone skipped their nap" (implying they are tired)
- "make the familiar strange" [p. 74]
    - That's how we get the best insights
- Our "agent detection device" is on a "hair trigger" [p. 76]
    - We see other agents everywhere, even when there isn't one
    - Not all agents need to be human, nor do they need to be living. [[The Intentional Stance]] explains how to use folk psychology to understand complex problems.
