> [!QUOTE] p. 86 The Personal/Sub-Personal Distinction
> Your eyes don't see; you do. Your mouth doesn't enjoy chocolate cake; you do. Your brain doesn't abhor the stinging pain in your shoulder; you do. Your hand doesn't sign a contract; you do.
^personal-subpersonal-distinction

## Thoughts
- Semantics define interpretation. They're more important than some people seem to think. [p. 86]
- Even with [[#^personal-subpersonal-distinction]], without eyes, we cannot see. Without eyes, we cannot hear, etc. [p. 87]
- Everything in the body above atoms is, to some degree, an agent [p. 88-89]
    - These are the "sub-personal parts"
    - They can be analyzed using [[The Intentional Stance]]
    - I'd argue atoms can also be analyzed using [[The Intentional Stance]]: they are loose/lower "agents" in the same way molecules and proteins are. Some atoms "want" to bond with others.
    - The further from atoms we go, the more complex the agent and its decisions (but also its capabilities)
- Analyzing something by decomposing into subpersonal parts (top-down) or from "atoms up" (bottom-up) can be beneficial
    - You don't have to start/stop at a human-- you can target any level
    - What are humans a subpersonal part of?
        - Society
        - Cities
        - World
        - Religion
        - A People
