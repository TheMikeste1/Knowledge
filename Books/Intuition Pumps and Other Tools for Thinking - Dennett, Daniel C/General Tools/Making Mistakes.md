## Thoughts
- Richard Feynman said we must publish both good and bad results [p. 19]
- Learn history to understand past (tempting) mistakes [p. 19]
    - This also avoids [[Chesterton's Fence]]
    - This applies to technical topics as well: what was the predecessor to the [[Neural Network|Multilayer Perceptron]]? What other options existed? Why did we go more towards [[Neural Network|NNs]] instead of the others?
- Sometimes we *want* to make mistakes. [p. 20] and [[#^the-key-to-progress]]
- Instead of pacing around with writer's block, "blurt it out!" [p. 20]
- The [[#^philosophy-definition]] applies to multiple fields, not just thought experiments. Maybe technical discussion should lead with a "philosophy" phase?
- Evolution consists of errors (mutations) that eventually produce something new. Bad mutations/mistakes will die off. Good ones have a chance at survival. Most don't matter. [pp 21, 22]
- Examine your mistakes instead of turning a way from them. Perform an autopsy, even if it's just a light one. Prepare for the autopsy by making [[#^clear-mistakes]]. Maybe it's worth documenting (might need a new vault section?)? [p. 22]
