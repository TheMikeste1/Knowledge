**Abstract**:: In this book, leading philosopher and best-selling author Daniel C. Dennett shares his favorite tools about tough questions. He begins with familiar examples-- Occam's Razor, Plato's Cave-- but quickly introduces original creations that unlock difficult problems, with colorful names such as *The Whimsical Jailer* and *Zombies and Zimboes*. Dennett's entertaining and illuminating thought experiments spark the imagination and spur heated discussions on the nature of free will, human consciousness, and our search for meaning.

**Notes**:: "Intuition pumps" are parables you can tweak to extract lessons and meaning

**Thoughts**:: Dr. Dennett starts off strong with some good tools and ways of thinking. I found some of the tools more applicable and useful than others, but otherwise generally useful. I diligently read up to An Interlude about Computers, at which point I continued to read until it became apparent I was already very familiar with the concepts he was laying forth. I still skimmed this section, but this is where the trouble for me began. While the computer section was just fine, subsequent sections I feel completely lost their charm. Almost every entry after the computer section were not so much tools as they were tearing down other people's work Dr. Dennett disagreed with or Dr. Dennett expressing his own opinions as fact. I did not extract much, if anything from these chapters, so I did not include notes here. There were still some lessons to be learned, but they were not worth penning rather than internalizing. Had the remainder of the book been similar to the first few chapters, it likely would have been a much better read.
