**Description**:: Lennox tackles humanity's attempt to create a "*Homo Deus*," meaning a god-like human. He primarily covers concerns on Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), making a distinction between it an "narrow" artificial intelligence that are common today. He attacks the idea and illustrates the futility (and danger) of attempting to make humans gods.

**Thoughts**:: In the first few chapters, Lennox relentlessly attacks an article which becomes so irrelevant by then end the of the book that I've forgotten the author and name. During this time, I feel he loses some of his credibility as he shows a profound misunderstanding of the scientific process and resorts to arguments that can easily be applied to counter his own philosophy. While I certainly agree with many of his statements, this behavior in the first few chapters made it extremely difficult for me to push through the remainder of the book. Additionally, his explanation of AI is fairly rudimentary and occasionally incorrect, but I am likely not the target audience for his explanation.<br>Once I was able to get past the first few chapters, Lennox exposes some interesting developments in AI. He also makes (hesitant-feeling) claim that the beast in Revelations and Kind David's similar vision might be AGI, or a man controlling the world through AGI. I felt this claim was weak at best, but Lennox did not seem to be as focused on AGI at this point and may not have been convinced himself.<br>Finally, Lennox ends with how we do not need a *Homo Deus* because the faithful will be resurrected and partake in His glory (my summary, not his). Of particular interest to me was the idea that Adam and Eve learned what good and evil are through the act of disobedience, i.e. they had always obeyed before so disobeying made the realize the difference between the two. This helps resolve some of the "magic fruit" feelings I occasionally have about their story (though there is likely still more to it than just that). Lennox ends with his testimony of the Gospel and encourages readers to be faithful.
