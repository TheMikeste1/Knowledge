## Definition

Voltage is the potential energy of electrical charges.

Just like how a boulder has potential energy at the top of the mountain due to gravity, electrons have potential energy due to the battery. The higher the "mountain," or voltage, the more potential energy the electron has. Additionally, just like how a boulder can smash into trees and obstacles in its path down the mountain (read: the boulder can have work on the way down), an electron can flow through circuits and capacitors as its work. As such, the amount of work an electron can perform is determined by the voltage.

The [[Current|conventional current]] direction is towards the top of the voltage mountain.

The change in potential energy $\Delta{U}$ of a charged particle $q$ and voltage $V$ can be represented as $\Delta{U} = qV$. Logically then, we can express voltage $V$ as the change in potential energy $\Delta{U}$ of a charged particle $q$ as $V = \frac{\Delta{U}}{q}$. Note that this voltage is the voltage between two points, just like how the potential energy of a boulder on a mountain is measured between the position of the boulder on the mountain and the base of the mountain.

Voltage is measured in volts $V$ (or $v$), which is equivalent to $1V = 1 \text{watt} / \text{ampere} = 1 \text{joule} / \text{coulomb}$.
