## Definition

Current is the flow of [[Charge|charge]]. Current is caused by electric or magnetic forces. Charges flow in a current, providing usable energy. Often, the charges used are electrons. However, some materials such as saltwater use both positive and negative charges. Current flows from higher to lower [[voltage]].

We measure current by observing how many charges, regardless of the sign of the charges, pass one spot in a some amount of time. For example, if measured over a second current would be charges per second, or $q / \sec$

Positive current flows in the direction of positive charges. Said another way, positive current flows in the direction opposite to which the electrons are flowing. For example, if ten electrons are moving to the left, we say there is a positive ten current to the right. This is called the "conventional current direction," or just "current." Sometimes "electron current" is used instead which is in the direction the electrons are flowing, but electron current is always explicitly called "electron current" while "conventional current" is usually just called "current."

Since current $i$ is a change over time, it can be represented as the equation
$$
i = \frac{dq}{dt}
$$

Current is measured in amperes, which comes from a study in magnetism. The [definition of an ampere](https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/ampere) is somewhat complicated, but is roughly equivalent to $1A \approx 6.7896868 \times 10^8 e \Delta{v_{Cs}}$ ($\approx 7e$ change in voltage coloumb seconds, where $e$ is the elementary charge of an electron).
